## 1. Overall Journey Architecture

### 1.1 The Hybrid Model: "Structured Flexibility"

Based on competitor research, we adopt a **hybrid approach** that provides:
- ✅ **Clear default path** for beginners who want guidance
- ✅ **Unit selection freedom** for experienced learners
- ✅ **Multiple entry points** via placement testing
- ✅ **Side content** for specific interests

**Visual Structure:**
```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         MAIN STORY PATH                 │
│   (Linear, CEFR-aligned, recommended)   │
│                                         │
│  A1 ─→ A2 ─→ B1 ─→ B2 ─→ (C1)         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
              ↕
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         SIDE QUESTS                     │
│   (Non-linear, thematic, optional)      │
│                                         │
│  Travel | Business | Culture | Stories  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
              ↕
┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│         PRACTICE HUB                    │
│   (Always accessible, review-focused)   │
│                                         │
│  Vocabulary | Grammar | Speaking | AI   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 1.2 Navigation Principles

**Default Experience (Recommended):**
- "Continue" button shows next recommended lesson
- Clear progress visualization on main path
- Like Duolingo: Zero decision fatigue

**Advanced Options:**
- Tap current unit to see level map
- Select any unit within unlocked levels
- Skip ahead via checkpoint tests
- Like Babbel/Busuu: Freedom to explore

**Smart Unlocking System:**
```
Complete 5 units in sequence
    → Automatic progression to next section

OR

Pass checkpoint test (75% threshold)
    → Skip ahead to desired section
```

This balances structure (for beginners) with flexibility (for motivated learners).

## 2. CEFR-Aligned Level Progression

### 2.1 Why CEFR?

The Common European Framework of Reference provides:
- ✅ **International standard** recognized globally
- ✅ **Clear milestones** at each level
- ✅ **Certification potential** (like Busuu)
- ✅ **Transparent benchmarking** against other resources

### 2.2 Linguino Level Structure

| CEFR Level | Linguino Name | Units | Hours | Key Milestone |
|------------|---------------|-------|-------|---------------|
| **A1** | Beginner | 30-35 | 40-60 | Survival communication |
| **A2** | Elementary | 35-40 | 60-90 | Basic conversations |
| **B1** | Intermediate | 40-50 | 90-120 | Independent user |
| **B2** | Upper-Int. | 50-60 | 120-180 | Fluent discussions |
| **C1** | Advanced | 40+ | 180+ | Near-native precision |

**Total to B2:** ~180-200 units, ~350-450 hours (comparable to university degree)

### 2.3 Level Definitions

#### A1 - Beginner (Breakthrough)
**Can-Do Statements:**
- Understand and use familiar everyday expressions
- Introduce myself and others
- Ask and answer simple personal questions
- Interact in a simple way if other person speaks slowly

**Grammar Focus:**
- Present tense (verb "to be," regular verbs)
- Basic word order (SVO)
- Simple questions and negation
- Personal pronouns, possessive adjectives
- Common prepositions (place, time)

**Vocabulary Target:** 600-800 high-frequency words

**Example Topics:**
- Greetings, introductions, farewells
- Numbers, colors, days, months
- Family members, personal descriptions
- Daily routine, food, home basics
- Basic travel phrases

---

#### A2 - Elementary (Waystage)
**Can-Do Statements:**
- Understand sentences on familiar topics
- Communicate in routine tasks
- Describe background and immediate environment
- Express matters of immediate need

**Grammar Focus:**
- Past simple (regular and irregular)
- Future forms ("going to," "will")
- Comparatives and superlatives
- More complex question types
- Modal verbs (can, should, must)

**Vocabulary Target:** 1,200-1,800 words total

**Example Topics:**
- Past experiences, childhood memories
- Future plans and goals
- Shopping, restaurants, services
- Health and body
- Hobbies and entertainment

---

#### B1 - Intermediate (Threshold)
**Can-Do Statements:**
- Understand main points on familiar matters
- Deal with most travel situations
- Produce simple connected text
- Describe experiences, events, dreams, hopes, ambitions
- Give reasons and explanations

**Grammar Focus:**
- Present perfect (vs. past simple)
- Passive voice (introduction)
- Conditional sentences (first, second)
- Indirect questions
- Relative clauses

**Vocabulary Target:** 2,500-3,500 words total

**Example Topics:**
- Work and education experiences
- Abstract concepts (environment, technology)
- Social issues and opinions
- Travel adventures
- Personal goals and aspirations

---

#### B2 - Upper-Intermediate (Vantage)
**Can-Do Statements:**
- Understand main ideas of complex text
- Interact with degree of fluency and spontaneity
- Produce clear, detailed text on wide range of subjects
- Explain viewpoint on topical issues

**Grammar Focus:**
- Advanced conditionals (third, mixed)
- Complex passive constructions
- Reported speech
- Various tense combinations
- Subjunctive mood (language-dependent)

**Vocabulary Target:** 4,500-6,000 words total

**Example Topics:**
- Professional communication
- Cultural discussions
- Abstract and philosophical topics
- Current events and debates
- Literature, arts, media

---

#### C1 - Advanced (Effective Operational Proficiency)
**Can-Do Statements:**
- Understand wide range of demanding, longer texts
- Express ideas fluently and spontaneously
- Use language flexibly and effectively
- Produce clear, well-structured, detailed text

**Grammar Focus:**
- Stylistic nuances
- Advanced idiomatic expressions
- Register and formality control
- Complex literary structures

**Vocabulary Target:** 7,000+ words

**Note:** C1 is aspirational. Most apps stop at B2. We can add C1 later based on demand.

## 3. The Three-Path System

### 3.1 Main Story Path (Primary Learning Journey)

**Purpose:** Core curriculum providing systematic CEFR progression

**Structure:**
```
SECTION 1: A1 Foundation (Units 1-35)
├── Chapter 1: First Words (Units 1-7)
│   ├── Unit 1: Greetings and Introductions
│   ├── Unit 2: Numbers and Colors
│   ├── Unit 3: Basic Questions
│   ├── Unit 4: Family Members
│   ├── Unit 5: Describing People
│   ├── Unit 6: Daily Activities
│   └── Unit 7: Review & Checkpoint
│
├── Chapter 2: Personal World (Units 8-14)
│   ├── Unit 8: My Home
│   ├── Unit 9: Food and Drinks
│   ├── Unit 10: Time and Schedules
│   ├── Unit 11: Clothes and Weather
│   ├── Unit 12: Hobbies and Free Time
│   ├── Unit 13: Places in Town
│   └── Unit 14: Review & Checkpoint
│
├── Chapter 3: Communication Basics (Units 15-21)
│   ├── Unit 15: At the Restaurant
│   ├── Unit 16: Shopping Essentials
│   ├── Unit 17: Directions and Transport
│   ├── Unit 18: Making Plans
│   ├── Unit 19: Expressing Preferences
│   ├── Unit 20: Simple Past Events
│   └── Unit 21: Review & Checkpoint
│
└── [Additional chapters continue...]

SECTION 2: A2 Building (Units 36-75)
SECTION 3: B1 Independence (Units 76-125)
SECTION 4: B2 Fluency (Units 126-185)
```

**Unit Composition:**
- **3-6 lessons per unit** (like Babbel: 5-15 min each)
- **Mixed lesson types:** vocabulary intro, grammar focus, practice, conversation, review
- **Checkpoint every 7 units:** Assessment + certificate eligibility

**Progression Logic:**
- Each unit builds on previous (prerequisite sequencing)
- Grammar introduced systematically (teachability hypothesis)
- Vocabulary spirals (revisited at increasing complexity)
- Topics progress: concrete → abstract

### 4.2 Topic Spiraling Strategy

**Key Principle:** Topics return at increasing complexity levels

**Example: "Food" Spiral**

**A1 - Unit 9:** Basic Food Vocabulary
- Apple, bread, water, coffee
- "I like pizza" / "I don't like fish"
- Present simple, common foods only

**A2 - Unit 22:** Ordering at a Restaurant
- Menu vocabulary, dishes, dietary needs
- "I'd like..." / "Could I have..."
- Polite requests, restaurant scenarios

**B1 - Unit 48:** Cooking and Recipes
- Cooking verbs, ingredients, measurements
- Imperative mood, sequencing
- Recipe reading, discussing cuisine

**B2 - Unit 103:** Food Culture and Traditions
- Culinary history, regional dishes
- Abstract discussion, opinions
- Cultural comparisons, gastronomic terminology

**Benefit:** Users see familiar topics with fresh complexity, maintaining interest while reinforcing foundations.

## 5. Grammar Progression Map

### 5.1 Sequencing Principles

**Teachability Hypothesis (Pienemann):**
> Grammar should be taught in the order learners naturally acquire it. Teaching too advanced structures too early is ineffective.

**Linguino Grammar Sequence:**

#### A1 Grammar Roadmap

**Foundational Structures:**
1. **Verb "to be"** - Present tense (am/is/are)
   - Personal pronouns (I, you, he, she, it, we, they)
   - Positive, negative, questions

2. **Subject-Verb-Object Word Order**
   - Basic sentence structure
   - Statement construction

3. **Present Simple** - Regular verbs
   - Affirmative sentences
   - Third-person -s/-es
   - Negative with do/does not
   - Yes/No questions

4. **Articles** - a/an/the (or equivalents)
   - Definite vs. indefinite
   - When to use/omit

5. **Possessive Adjectives**
   - my, your, his, her, its, our, their
   - Expressing ownership

6. **Adjectives** - Common descriptive words
   - Position in sentence (before noun or after verb)
   - Basic adjective agreement (language-dependent)

7. **Demonstratives** - this/that/these/those
   - Pointing out objects and people

8. **Prepositions of Place** - in, on, at, under, next to
   - Describing location

9. **Prepositions of Time** - at, on, in
   - Talking about when things happen

10. **Numbers and Quantities**
    - Cardinal numbers (1-100+)
    - Basic quantifiers (some, any, much, many)

11. **Basic Questions**
    - Who, what, where, when, how
    - Question word order

12. **Simple Imperatives**
    - Commands and instructions
    - Affirmative and negative

---

#### A2 Grammar Roadmap

**Expanding Complexity:**

1. **Past Simple** - Regular verbs
   - -ed endings
   - Affirmative, negative, questions

2. **Past Simple** - Irregular verbs
   - Common irregular forms (go-went, see-saw)
   - Mixed practice

3. **Past Simple of "to be"** - was/were

4. **Future "going to"**
   - Plans and intentions
   - Predictions based on evidence

5. **Future "will"**
   - Promises, offers, spontaneous decisions
   - Predictions

6. **Present Continuous**
   - Actions happening now
   - be + -ing form
   - Contrast with present simple

7. **Modal Verbs: can/can't**
   - Ability and permission
   - Requests

8. **Modal Verbs: should/shouldn't**
   - Advice and recommendations

9. **Modal Verbs: must/have to**
   - Obligation and necessity

10. **Comparatives and Superlatives**
    - Regular forms (-er/-est, more/most)
    - Irregular forms (good-better-best)

11. **Quantifiers**
    - much/many, a few/a little, a lot of

12. **Adverbs of Frequency**
    - always, usually, often, sometimes, never
    - Position in sentence

13. **Object Pronouns**
    - me, you, him, her, it, us, them

14. **Possessive Pronouns**
    - mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs

---

#### B1 Grammar Roadmap

**Moving to Complex Structures:**

1. **Present Perfect** - Experiences
   - have/has + past participle
   - ever, never, before, already, yet

2. **Present Perfect vs. Past Simple**
   - Finished time vs. unfinished time
   - Critical distinction

3. **Present Perfect Continuous**
   - Duration up to now
   - Emphasis on activity

4. **Past Continuous**
   - Actions in progress in the past
   - Setting scenes, interrupted actions

5. **Past Continuous vs. Past Simple**
   - Background vs. main events

6. **Used to / Would**
   - Past habits and states

7. **First Conditional**
   - Real future possibilities
   - if + present simple, will

8. **Second Conditional**
   - Hypothetical present/future
   - if + past simple, would

9. **Passive Voice** - Present and past
   - Focus on action rather than doer
   - be + past participle

10. **Relative Clauses** - Defining
    - who, which, that, where, when
    - Essential information

11. **Relative Clauses** - Non-defining
    - Additional information with commas

12. **Indirect Questions**
    - Embedded questions
    - More polite forms
    - Word order changes

13. **Reported Speech** - Statements
    - Backshifting tenses
    - Reporting verbs (say, tell)

14. **Gerunds and Infinitives**
    - Verb patterns
    - like doing vs. like to do

---

#### B2 Grammar Roadmap

**Refining and Nuancing:**

1. **Third Conditional**
   - Hypothetical past
   - if + past perfect, would have

2. **Mixed Conditionals**
   - Past condition, present result (and vice versa)

3. **Wish / If only**
   - Regrets and desires

4. **Advanced Passive Constructions**
   - All tenses in passive
   - Passive with modals

5. **Causative Structures**
   - have/get something done

6. **Reported Speech** - Questions and commands
   - Backshifting in questions
   - Reporting imperatives

7. **Phrasal Verbs** - Systematic study
   - Separable vs. inseparable
   - Multiple meanings

8. **Advanced Modals**
   - Deduction (must, might, can't)
   - Past modals (should have, could have)

9. **Articles** - Advanced usage
   - Zero article
   - Specific vs. general

10. **Inversion** - For emphasis
    - Negative adverbials at beginning
    - Formal structures

11. **Cleft Sentences**
    - It is... that / What... is
    - Emphasis structures

12. **Subjunctive Mood** (language-dependent)
    - Wishes, suggestions, demands

---

### 5.2 Grammar Integration Strategy

**Never Teach Grammar in Isolation**

✅ **DO:**
- Introduce grammar within communicative context
- Use authentic examples from topics
- Provide brief explanations (30-60 seconds)
- Immediately apply in practice exercises
- Return to grammar points across multiple units (spiraling)

❌ **DON'T:**
- Create pure grammar lessons with no context
- Over-explain rules academically
- Expect mastery after one exposure
- Test grammar separately from communication

**Example Integration:**

**Unit 16: Shopping Essentials (A2)**
- **Topic:** Buying clothes at a store
- **Grammar Point:** Comparatives (cheaper than, more expensive than, better quality)
- **Integration:**
  - Dialogue: "This shirt is cheaper than that one"
  - Vocabulary: Price words, quality adjectives
  - Practice: Compare items in shopping scenarios
  - Production: Role-play comparing products

**Result:** Grammar learned through meaningful use, not as abstract rule.

## 6. Vocabulary Acquisition Strategy

### 6.1 Research-Based Principles

**Frequency Effect:**
- Teach **high-frequency words first**
- 2,000 most common words = 80% of everyday communication
- Use frequency dictionaries for each language pair

**Required Exposures:**
- **8-12 meaningful encounters** to establish word recognition
- **20-30 spaced repetitions** to move to long-term memory
- Multiple contexts enhance retention

**Vocabulary Thresholds:**
- **600-800 words** → A1 survival communication
- **1,500-1,800 words** → A2 basic conversations
- **2,500-3,500 words** → B1 independent user
- **4,500-6,000 words** → B2 fluent discussions
- **7,000+ words** → C1 advanced proficiency

### 6.2 Vocabulary Presentation Strategy

**First Encounter (Introduction):**
- Visual support (images, icons)
- Audio from native speaker (multiple speakers ideal)
- Example sentence showing usage
- Translation provided

**Subsequent Encounters (Reinforcement):**
- Recognition exercises (matching, multiple choice)
- Cloze exercises (fill in the blank)
- Sentence construction using word
- Listening comprehension featuring word
- Speaking practice with word

**Spaced Repetition Schedule:**
```
Day 1: Introduction (lesson content)
Day 2: First review (automatic insertion)
Day 4: Second review
Day 7: Third review
Day 14: Fourth review
Day 30: Fifth review
...continuing with expanding intervals
```

**Algorithm Adjustment:**
- Correct answer → increase interval
- Incorrect answer → reset to shorter interval
- Difficulty rating influences scheduling

### 6.3 Vocabulary Grouping Strategies

**Thematic Grouping:**
- Words related to a topic (food, travel, work)
- Easier to remember in context
- Supports communication goals

**Word Families:**
- work (verb) → worker (noun) → hardworking (adjective)
- Builds productive vocabulary efficiently

**Collocations:**
- Common word combinations
- "make a decision" not "do a decision"
- Native-like usage

**False Friends (Language-Specific):**
- EN-FR: "actually" ≠ "actuellement" (currently)
- EN-DE: "become" ≠ "bekommen" (to receive)
- Explicitly taught to prevent errors

## 7. Skills Balance

### 7.1 Receptive vs. Productive Skills

**Research Finding:**
> Receptive competence (understanding) develops FASTER than productive competence (speaking/writing). The gap must be actively bridged through output practice and feedback.

**Linguino's Approach: Active Bridging**

**Receptive Skills (Reading & Listening):**
- 40% of lesson time at A1-A2
- 35% of lesson time at B1-B2
- Heavy input provides foundation

**Productive Skills (Speaking & Writing):**
- 30% of lesson time at A1-A2
- 35% of lesson time at B1-B2
- Early output opportunities prevent passive knowledge

**Interactive Skills (Conversation & Communication):**
- 30% of lesson time at A1-A2
- 30% of lesson time at B1-B2
- Real-world application

### 7.2 Four Skills Breakdown

#### Reading
**Progression:**
- **A1:** Single words, short phrases, simple sentences
- **A2:** Short texts, dialogues, basic stories
- **B1:** Longer texts, articles, narratives
- **B2:** Complex texts, literature, analysis

**Activities:**
- Matching words to images
- Reading comprehension questions
- Fill in missing words
- Ordering sentences
- Summarizing passages

---

#### Listening
**Progression:**
- **A1:** Slow, clear native speech, familiar topics
- **A2:** Normal pace on routine topics
- **B1:** Native speed, various accents, extended speech
- **B2:** Fast speech, idioms, complex topics

**Activities:**
- Listen and select (multiple choice)
- Dictation exercises
- Fill in transcript gaps
- Comprehension questions
- Dialogue shadowing

**Key Feature:**
> Multiple native speakers from different regions (not synthesized voices). Talker variability improves acquisition.

---

#### Writing
**Progression:**
- **A1:** Single words, copying, simple sentences
- **A2:** Short paragraphs, basic messages
- **B1:** Connected texts, emails, narratives
- **B2:** Essays, formal writing, creative texts

**Activities:**
- Sentence construction (word tiles)
- Fill in the blank
- Translation exercises
- Guided writing prompts
- Free writing with feedback

**AI Integration:**
- GPT-based feedback on open-ended responses
- Grammar correction with explanations
- Alternative phrasing suggestions
- Not just right/wrong, but improvement guidance

---

#### Speaking
**Progression:**
- **A1:** Individual words, memorized phrases
- **A2:** Simple sentences, basic conversations
- **B1:** Connected speech, spontaneous dialogue
- **B2:** Fluent, nuanced, extended speech

**Activities:**
- Pronunciation practice (speech recognition)
- Sentence repetition (shadowing)
- Fill in dialogue gaps (voice input)
- Role-play scenarios with AI
- Open conversation practice

**AI Conversation Partner:**
- GPT-powered chatbot (like Memrise MemBot)
- Scenario-based conversations
- Adaptive to user level
- Provides corrections and encouragement
- Available in Practice Hub for unlimited practice

**Key Differentiator:**
> Speaking practice from day one, not locked behind paywall or limited by "lives."

## 8. Language-Specific Considerations

### 8.1 EN → DE (English to German)

**Key Challenges:**
- **Grammatical gender** (der/die/das - 3 genders)
- **Four cases** (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive)
- **Verb position** (V2 in main clauses, verb-final in subordinate clauses)
- **Compound words** (Donaudampfschifffahrt)
- **Separable verbs** (aufstehen → Ich stehe auf)

**Pedagogical Adaptations:**

**A1 Focus:**
- Introduce gender early (always teach nouns with articles)
- Nominative case first (subjects)
- Fixed word order patterns before explaining V2
- Common separable verbs (aufstehen, einkaufen)

**A2 Focus:**
- Accusative case (direct objects)
- Dative case (indirect objects)
- Two-way prepositions (motion vs. location)
- Past tense (sein/haben + participle)

**B1 Focus:**
- Genitive case (possession)
- Subordinating conjunctions (verb-final word order)
- Reflexive verbs
- Adjective endings (full paradigm)

**B2 Focus:**
- Subjunctive II (Konjunktiv II)
- Passive constructions
- Extended adjective phrases
- Register and formality

**Vocabulary Strategy:**
- Always present nouns with article AND plural form
- Color-code genders (der=blue, die=red, das=green) in early lessons
- Group words by gender patterns where possible
- Emphasize cognates (Hand-hand, Haus-house) but watch false friends (Gift≠gift)

**Cultural Integration:**
- Formal vs. informal address (Sie vs. du) - critical from A1
- Regional differences (Swiss, Austrian, German German)
- Compound word building as cultural insight

---

### 8.2 DE → EN (German to English)

**Key Challenges:**
- **Phrasal verbs** (put up with, get over, run into)
- **Articles** (the vs. a vs. zero article - less systematic than German)
- **Tense usage** (present perfect vs. past simple distinction)
- **Pronunciation** (th-sounds, r-sounds, vowel length)
- **Idioms** (raining cats and dogs, piece of cake)

**Pedagogical Adaptations:**

**A1 Focus:**
- Simpler grammar advantage (no cases, simpler gender system)
- Pronunciation focus (th, w/v distinction, r)
- Basic phrasal verbs (get up, sit down)
- Present simple and continuous distinction

**A2 Focus:**
- Past simple vs. present perfect (German uses Perfekt for both)
- Article usage patterns
- Common phrasal verbs
- Question formation (auxiliary verbs)

**B1 Focus:**
- Phrasal verb mastery (separable like German, but more idiomatic)
- Gerunds and infinitives (like doing vs. like to do)
- Tense distinctions (present perfect continuous, past perfect)
- Idioms and expressions

**B2 Focus:**
- Subtle tense differences
- Advanced phrasal verbs
- Register and formality
- British vs. American English

**Vocabulary Strategy:**
- Cognates helpful (Butter=butter, Computer=computer)
- False friends (aktuell≠actual, bekommen≠become)
- Phrasal verbs taught systematically, not all at once
- Idiomatic expressions for natural speech

**Cultural Integration:**
- British vs. American variants (both presented)
- Informal communication norms (less formal than German)
- Cultural references (sports, holidays, media)

---

### 8.3 DE → ES (German to Spanish)

**Key Challenges:**
- **Different grammatical systems** (cases vs. conjugations)
- **Verb conjugations** (6 forms per tense, many tenses)
- **Gender** (2 genders: masculine/feminine, but different from German)
- **Ser vs. Estar** (two "to be" verbs)
- **Subjunctive mood** (extensively used)

**Pedagogical Adaptations:**

**A1 Focus:**
- Rebuild gender intuition (das Haus→la casa, der Tisch→la mesa - not matching)
- Present tense conjugations (6 forms)
- Ser vs. estar distinction immediately
- Pronunciation (consistent phonetics - advantage!)

**A2 Focus:**
- Past tenses (preterite and imperfect distinction)
- Reflexive verbs (similar to German, but more common)
- Gustar construction (indirect object required)
- Por vs. para (preposition distinction)

**B1 Focus:**
- Present subjunctive (uncertainty, wishes, emotions)
- Present perfect (recent past)
- Past subjunctive
- Imperative forms

**B2 Focus:**
- Future and conditional
- All subjunctive tenses
- Passive constructions (se + verb)
- Regional variations (Spain vs. Latin America)

**Vocabulary Strategy:**
- Some cognates (Telefon→teléfono, Universität→universidad)
- But mostly new vocabulary (no Germanic roots)
- Gender doesn't transfer (das Mädchen→la chica, die Sonne→el sol)
- Emphasize high-frequency words early

**Cultural Integration:**
- Formal/informal address (tú vs. usted, like Sie/du)
- Regional differences (vosotros in Spain, ustedes in Latin America)
- Cultural richness (Spain, Mexico, Argentina, etc.)

---

### 8.4 EN → FR (English to French)

**Key Challenges:**
- **Grammatical gender** (le/la - 2 genders)
- **Verb conjugations** (complex, irregular)
- **Pronunciation** (nasal vowels, silent letters, liaison)
- **False friends** (very common: libraire≠library)
- **Formal/informal** (tu vs. vous)

**Pedagogical Adaptations:**

**A1 Focus:**
- Gender with all nouns (le livre, la table)
- Present tense conjugations (irregular avoir, être, aller)
- Pronunciation (nasal sounds: an, on, in, un)
- Articles (le/la/les, un/une/des)
- Basic adjective agreement

**A2 Focus:**
- Past tenses (passé composé with avoir/être)
- Imparfait (imperfect)
- Reflexive verbs (se lever, se coucher)
- Adjective placement (before vs. after noun)
- Partitive articles (du, de la, des)

**B1 Focus:**
- Subjunctive mood (il faut que...)
- Relative pronouns (qui, que, dont, où)
- Conditional tense
- Object pronouns (me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les)
- Y and en pronouns

**B2 Focus:**
- Past subjunctive
- Literary tenses (passé simple - recognition)
- Complex pronoun combinations
- Gérondif (en + participle)
- Register and formality

**Vocabulary Strategy:**
- MANY cognates (restaurant, information, général)
- But false friends critical (actuellement=currently, attendre=to wait)
- Pronunciation often different despite spelling (restaurant, garage)
- Idiomatic expressions essential for fluency

**Cultural Integration:**
- Formal/informal address (vous vs. tu) - more formal than English
- French politeness rituals (bonjour, s'il vous plaît, merci)
- Regional accents (Parisian, Québécois, Belgian, etc.)
- Cultural richness (cuisine, art, philosophy)

---

### 8.5 Cross-Language Insights

**Leverage Transfer:**
- Cognates between languages (EN-FR especially rich)
- Grammatical concepts that exist in multiple languages (gender in DE-FR-ES)
- Help learners recognize patterns across languages they know

**Warn Against Negative Transfer:**
- False friends explicitly taught
- Different grammatical systems explained (not all languages work like English)
- Pronunciation differences highlighted

**Polyglot Features:**
- Allow users to indicate languages they already know
- Provide comparison notes ("Spanish ser/estar is like...")
- Leverage existing knowledge for faster acquisition

### 9.1 Research-Based Motivation Mechanics

**Streak Counter (HIGHEST IMPACT):**
- **3.6x engagement increase** for 7-day streaks (Duolingo data)
- Daily lesson completion maintains streak
- Streak freeze available (1-2 per month) for fairness
- Milestone celebrations (7, 30, 100, 365 days)

**Visual Progress:**
- Path completion percentage
- Circular progress rings on individual lessons
- Unit completion badges
- Level advancement celebrations

**Points and Achievements:**
- XP per lesson completed
- Daily XP goals (customizable: 10, 20, 50 XP)
- Achievements for milestones (not overwhelming)
- Weekly progress summary

**CEFR Certificates:**
- Earn official-looking certificate upon level completion
- Downloadable PDF (like Busuu)
- Shareable on social media
- Motivates long-term goal

### 9.2 What We WON'T Do (Anti-Patterns)

❌ **Lives/Hearts System** (Duolingo's most hated feature)
- Penalizes mistakes
- Creates anxiety
- Discourages experimentation
- Pushes monetization

❌ **Aggressive Monetization**
- No artificial barriers to free content
- No disruptive ads mid-lesson
- No constant upgrade prompts

❌ **Over-Gamification**
- No meaningless badges for everything
- No pushy notifications ("Your streak is dying!")
- No manipulative dark patterns

✅ **Generous Free Tier**
- Full access to Main Story Path (A1-B2)
- Side Quests and Practice Hub included
- Only premium: Offline mode, advanced analytics, tutor support (future)

### 9.3 Social & Community Features

**Optional, Not Required:**
- Weekly leagues (opt-in, like Duolingo)

**Community Learning:**

### 10.5 Content Creation Workflow

**For Each Unit:**

1. **Define Learning Objectives**
   - CEFR can-do statements
   - Grammar point(s) to introduce/reinforce
   - Vocabulary target (15-25 new words)
   - Communicative goal (what can user DO after this unit?)

2. **Select Topic & Context**
   - Follows topic sequencing framework
   - Culturally relevant and interesting
   - Authentic materials where possible

3. **Create Lesson Sequence (5 lessons typical):**
   - **Lesson 1:** Vocabulary introduction + context
   - **Lesson 2:** Grammar explanation + controlled practice
   - **Lesson 3:** Mixed practice (reading, listening)
   - **Lesson 4:** Production (writing, speaking)
   - **Lesson 5:** Review and consolidation

4. **Develop Exercises**
   - 15-25 exercises per lesson
   - Mixed types for engagement
   - Increasing difficulty within lesson

5. **Record Audio**
   - Native speakers (male and female)
   - Clear pronunciation
   - Natural pace

6. **QA & Testing**
   - Internal testing for clarity
   - User testing (sample group)
   - Iterate based on feedback

7. **Integrate Spaced Repetition**
   - Tag vocabulary for review algorithm
   - Set difficulty ratings
   - Link to related content

**Content Team Structure:**
- Language experts (native speakers, teachers)
- Pedagogical designers
- Exercise developers
- Audio producers
- QA testers
